Accessibility shouldn't need a workaround

hCaptcha challenges are hard for everyone, and hardest for people using screen readers, keyboard navigation or switch devices. hCaptcha's answer is an accessibility cookie you have to request and users have to opt into: a workaround layered on top of a wall.

oopspam never shows a challenge, so there's nothing to work around. Forms stay usable for everyone and you stop losing visitors at the last step. We wrote about this in CAPTCHA and accessibility.

No widget, no script, no visitor cookies

hCaptcha is more privacy-conscious than reCAPTCHA, but it's still a widget: a third-party script loads on every form, sets its own cookies, and sees every visitor. oopspam runs entirely server-side: no widget, no front-end script, and no visitor cookies to disclose.

We keep no logs by default and ask for the minimum data needed to score a submission.

Faster forms, no widget

Every hCaptcha challenge means loading hCaptcha's script and doing extra work in the browser before a form can be submitted. Our tests show it adds real time to form pages. See the hCaptcha performance analysis.

oopspam adds nothing to the page: the check happens on your server after submission, so your forms stay as fast as your theme.

Zero challenges for your visitors

oopspam returns a spam score for every submission and lets you tune the sensitivity to avoid false positives. You can block by country or language when an attack hits. The difference is that your visitors never see any of it.

A report for every block

hCaptcha gives you a pass/fail and little explanation. oopspam sends a detailed report for every blocked submission so you can see which signals fired and rescue any legitimate message that tripped the filter.
